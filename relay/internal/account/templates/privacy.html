Privacy Policy

Effective {{.Data.Effective}}

This policy says what this Latchkey relay knows about you, what it is used for, and how long it is kept. This relay is run by {{.Data.Name}} ("we", "us"). The policy covers this relay. The Latchkey app on your Mac, and Claude, are mentioned where they matter.

The short version

  • The relay stores what it needs to sign you in, to find your Mac, and to show you what happened. All of it is listed below.
  • The relay keeps the full content of every tool call Claude makes through it: what Claude asked your machines (your notes, messages, files, screen contents, commands) and everything they answered, screenshots and other images included. It is stored encrypted, kept until you delete your account, and you can download all of it from your account page.
  • We do not sell your data. There is no advertising, no tracking, and no analytics.
  • Delete account, on your account page, removes what the relay stores about you at once, the full activity included.

What the relay stores

About your account

  • Your email address, when you signed up and when you confirmed the address, and how many wrong sign-in codes were entered for it today.
  • Your passkeys' public keys, with what a passkey check needs: an identifier, a use counter, the kind of authenticator that made it, and when it was created and last used. The private part of a passkey never leaves your devices. There are no passwords.
  • A record of each browser you are signed in with: a hashed form of its session cookie, when it was created, when it expires, and when you last confirmed with your passkey. That cookie is the only one this site sets.
  • The codes we email you for sign-up and recovery, in hashed form, for 15 minutes. That a recovery code was used is kept for 30 days, so your Mac can tell you about it.

About your machines

  • Each machine's name, whether it is a Mac or a Linux server, the public key it identifies itself with, the versions of Latchkey and of its operating system it reported, and when it was connected to your account. Its private key never leaves the machine.
  • Each machine's last reported state: whether it is connected, when it connected and was last seen, and why it went offline if it said so; for a Mac also its battery level, power source, whether it is charging and how long the battery should last, how warm it is running, low power mode, whether the lid is closed, its time zone, and whether keep-awake is on; for a Linux server how long it has been running and its time zone. Only the latest state is kept, not a history.
  • The list of tools each machine offers to Claude (their names and descriptions), which follows what you turned on with Latchkey on that machine.
  • The one-time code that connects your Mac to your account, in hashed form, for 5 minutes.
  • The request a Linux server makes to be connected: the name, kind, and public key it sent, the codes it showed you (in hashed form), and whether and by whom it was approved, for an hour after its 10 minutes run out.

About Claude's access

  • Which Claude apps you approved: for each approval, the app's name and web address as you saw them when you approved it, where the approval was sent, when you gave it, and when it was last used.
  • The sign-in codes and access tokens issued for those approvals, kept as signatures that can't be used to sign in, with their expiry times.
  • A description of each Claude app that asked to connect: its name and where it sends people back to, as the app published or registered it. This describes the app, not you.
  • A note of each approval, for 30 days, so that your Mac can notify you of it once.

About what happened

  • Problem reports to Latchkey's developer, only if you turned on sharing them in the Latchkey app: when Claude finds that something it did through Latchkey didn't work, it writes a report (what went wrong, without your passwords, codes, or other private details). The report is encrypted so that only Latchkey's developer can read it, and kept here, encrypted, only until the developer's service accepts it, at most 7 days. It carries no account, email address, machine name, or relay address. Turning sharing off deletes any report not yet sent.
  • An activity log: for each tool call, an identifier of the call, which tool ran, when, which Claude app made the call (the name it gave), how long it took, and the outcome (worked, failed, timed out, or your Mac was offline). It holds no arguments and no results; those are in the full activity below. Removing an app's access from your Mac is logged the same way. Your Mac can ask for its part of this log, to check it against its own.
  • A record of events on your account, with the time and, where it applies, your Mac: each time your Mac connects and disconnects (and why), each change of keep-awake, each sign-in attempt and how it went (passkey, emailed code, or recovery), each Claude app you approved, refused, or that lost its access, each time a Mac was connected to or disconnected from your account, and each request the relay refused or failed, with the reason. Refused requests are counted per hour rather than one by one. The record never holds what Claude did on your Mac, codes, tokens, keys, or network addresses. Requests refused without any account are counted per hour in the same way, without anything that identifies who sent them.
  • Your full activity: for each tool call Claude made through the relay, including calls that never reached a machine and Claude's questions to the relay about your machines, what Claude sent (the tool and everything it asked for), the whole answer Claude got back (text, screenshots and other images, and the status of your machines that the relay adds), whether it failed, how long it took, the call's identifier, which Claude app made it, and which machine it went to; and the events on your account listed above. It is kept in files of one day each, encrypted with the relay's own key. Nothing in it is deleted because of its age. You can download all of it from your account page (Download full activity).

For running the relay

  • The relay's own secret keys, which sign tokens and protect forms, stored encrypted. They are not about you.
  • A list of blocked email addresses, if we blocked any because of abuse, with when and why.
  • A record of what the operator did to an account (suspending it, lifting a suspension, deleting it, blocking its address): the email address, what was done, the reason given, who did it, and when. Kept for one year, also when the account is gone.
  • Counters that limit abuse: how many requests came from a network address, for an account, or for an email address in the last minutes. They are held in memory only and are gone when the relay restarts.
  • The relay's own output, for troubleshooting: the events above, one line each (refused requests: the first of each hour), and error messages. They can hold the relay's internal identifier of an account or a Mac, and never content, codes, tokens, or network addresses.
  • If this relay runs behind the web server that Latchkey's own deployment sets up, that web server keeps an access log for 30 days: the network address a request came from, the web address requested (which can include your Mac's name when you connect it), the time, and technical details of the request such as the kind of browser or app. Cookies, tokens, and one-time codes are left out of it.

What the relay does not store

  • Your machines' own activity logs. Each Mac and Linux server keeps its own detailed log, including the content of tool calls, on that machine. It is not sent to the relay.
  • Passwords (there are none), the private part of your passkeys, and your Mac's private key.
  • Your conversations with Claude. Those are between you and Anthropic.

What can be seen in transit

Everything between Claude, the relay, and your Mac travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS). The relay is where those connections meet, and tool traffic is not encrypted beyond that. So the server this relay runs on can technically see tool traffic while it passes through, and the relay keeps it in your full activity, encrypted with the relay's own key; whoever runs the server holds that key. If that matters for what you let Claude do, you can run your own relay: the software is open source.

Claude is operated by Anthropic, under Anthropic's own terms and privacy policy. What you say to Claude, and what your Mac's tools return to Claude, is handled by Anthropic, not by us. With each answer from your Mac, the relay also tells Claude your Mac's status, such as its battery level and whether it is online.

What we use it for

  • To sign you in and keep your account safe.
  • To connect Claude to your Mac, and to tell Claude when your Mac is offline and why.
  • To show you your Mac's status, the apps that have access, and the activity log, and to let you download your full activity.
  • To find and fix what goes wrong and to improve how Latchkey works with Claude, by reviewing what happened in tool calls.
  • To email you sign-in and recovery codes, and a notice whenever a machine is connected to your account, an app is allowed to use your machines, or your account is recovered. Each notice names the network address the request came from; the relay doesn't keep that address.
  • To protect this relay from abuse and to keep it running.

Nothing else: no advertising, no profiles, no training of AI models.

Who else handles it

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it for advertising. It is handled only by {{if .Data.Providers}}the companies we need in order to run this relay: {{.Data.Providers}}{{else}}the operator's hosting and email providers{{end}}. They process it for us, to host the relay and to deliver its emails, so your email address and the emails we send you pass through the email provider. We may also have to disclose what the relay stores when the law requires it.

The server may be in a different country from the one you live in.

How long it is kept

  • Your account, passkeys, Mac details, and approvals: until you remove them or delete your account.
  • The activity log, the record of events, and your full activity: until you delete your account. Nothing in them is deleted because of its age. If the server's disk runs low, the relay stops adding to the full activity until there is room again, and deletes none of it.
  • Browser sign-ins: 30 days, or until you sign out.
  • Emailed codes: 15 minutes. The code that connects your Mac: 5 minutes. A sign-up that was never finished: one day.
  • Access tokens: one hour. The tokens that renew them: 90 days from their last renewal. Both are removed at once when you remove an app's access.
  • The notes of approvals and the web server's access log: 30 days.
  • The record of what the operator did: one year. A blocked address: until it is unblocked.

Delete account removes all of this at once and disconnects your Mac. Only a blocked address and the operator's record stay, if there are any.{{if .Data.BackupDays}} Copies of the server made for recovery are kept for {{.Data.BackupDays}} days, so deleted data can remain in such a backup for up to that long. Backups are used only to restore the relay after a failure.{{end}}

Security

You sign in with a passkey, never a password. Codes, session cookies, and tokens are stored only as hashes or signatures, never in a form that could be used to sign in. Connections are encrypted. Your Mac only ever connects outward to the relay, and proves who it is with a key kept in its secure hardware. No system is perfectly secure. If we learn that your data was exposed, we will tell you by email.

Children

This relay is not for anyone under 18, and we don't knowingly collect data from children.

We will not treat you differently for asking, and we will answer as soon as we reasonably can and within the time the law sets.

If European or UK data protection law applies to you: we handle your data because it is needed to provide the service you asked for and to keep it secure, and you can also complain to your data protection authority.
